Greetings!

I am a Ph.D. candidate at Leiden Observatory, Leiden University in the research group of Dr. Melissa McClure. My thesis focuses on probing the chemical building blocks of planetesimals in protoplanetary disks. I hope to use models and observations in the bridging of astrochemistry and planet formation to contribute to the big question of how planets, such as Earth, formed in the first place.

I earned my M.Sc. in Astronomy Research from Leiden University in 2025. My M.Sc. thesis was supervised by Dr. Melissa McClure, and my first-year research project was supervised by Dr. Xander Tielens. Previously, I graduated as an I.I. Rabi Scholar in 2022 from Columbia University where I recieved my B.A. — Major in Astronomy, Concentration in Chemistry. I was raised in New York City and am a city girl at heart, but I also enjoy getting away from the urban scene for an adventurous hike every once and awhile.

Research

Selected projects.
Check out my full CV for more details!

  • Chemical Modeling of the Innermost Disk
    (2025 – present)

    I am developing a physical-chemical model that predicts atomic line fluxes originating from the innermost protoplanetary disk regions. A significant depletion of volatile and/or refractory elements may provide evidence of trapping mechanisms farther out in the disk, such as planetesimal formation or dust traps.

  • Hot HCN and C2H2 Gas Toward W3 IRS5
    (2023 – 2024)

    As part of my first-year M.Sc. project at Leiden University, I studied hot HCN and C2H2 gas toward the massive hot core binary W3 IRS5. I analyzed the mid-infrared (MIR) absorption bands in order to characterize the binary's temperatures and densities.

  • Quasicrystalline Structure in Low-Temperature CO2 Ice
    (2022)

    Embedded in the Surface Dynamics Group at Aarhus University, I studied CO2 ice clusters deposited on highly oriented pyrolytic graphite (HOPG) with low temperature scanning tunneling microscopy (LT-STM). This work contributed to a paper submitted to Nature Communications.

  • Ice Formation and Grain Growth in Perseus and Serpens
    (2020 – 2022)

    At the Institute for Astronmy at the University of Hawai'i at Manoa, I investigated the 3 micron water ice and 9.7 micron silicate features observed toward background stars in the Perseus and Serpens molecular clouds. This project culminated in a first-author paper published in the Astrophysical Journal.
